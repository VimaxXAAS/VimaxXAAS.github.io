Research Vision

My research investigates the genetic architecture and evolutionary mechanisms governing fruit crop resilience and quality. Combining haplotype-resolved genome assemblies, transcriptomics, and untargeted metabolomics, my work deciphers how local grapevine germplasm in Central Asia adapted to climatic extremes and how phytohormonal crosstalk coordinates defenses against destructive fungal pathogens.

Core Research Themes

Grapevine Domestication & Genomics

Haplotype-resolved genome assembly and re-sequencing of ancient and regional Xinjiang germplasm (e.g., 'Munage') to reconstruct evolutionary lineages and trait selection.

Biotic Stress & Rootstock Crosstalk

Deciphering molecular signaling pathways, transcriptional reprogramming, and hormonal balances mitigating gray mold and powdery mildew pathogenesis in horticultural crops.

Berry Quality & Nutritional Metabolomics

Profiling secondary metabolites, sugar dynamics, and dehydration kinetics underlying raisin nutrition, berry cracking, and antioxidant capacity.

Functional Transcription Factors

Functional mining and characterization of candidate gene families (Trihelix, Dirigent, BPC, and Laccase) conferring resilience under abiotic and thermal fluctuations.
